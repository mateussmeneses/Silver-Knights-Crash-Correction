METHOD
  Attached to the Windows default render endpoint via WASAPI
  (IAudioMeterInformation::GetPeakValue over IMMDeviceEnumerator), then sampled the
  endpoint peak while the game ran. This measures signal actually arriving at the
  output device, so it distinguishes "audible" from "a DLL was loaded".

  The idle baseline is the control that validates the instrument: peak 0.00000 with
  no game running. Without that control, a non-zero reading could be another app.

INTERPRETATION
  * Menu BGM is real and CONTINUOUS: every one of 24 samples was non-zero, peak ~0.05.
    A steady low-level signal is exactly what looping background music looks like on a
    peak meter. This is the MCI / DirectShow MP3 path (QUARTZ.dll + l3codeca.acm +
    msmpeg2adec.dll + mciqtz32.dll were all confirmed loaded in run-17), consistent
    with the DirectShow FilterGraphWindow observed at T+6.7 s.
  * In-match audio saturates the meter (1.00000) with 24/24 non-zero samples. That is
    the DirectSound8 path (se\*.wav) plus the continuing BGM. A sustained 1.0 peak is
    not by itself proof of clean mixing - it can indicate clipping - so "audible" is
    established while "correct gain staging" is NOT.
  * Attacking did not change the meter from saturation, so this run does not isolate
    individual sound effects from the music bed. It shows the output pipeline is live,
    not that every effect plays.

WHAT IS PROVEN vs NOT
  PROVEN on Windows 11: the audio output pipeline produces real signal on both the
  menu/music path and in-match. Silent failure - the single most common Wine/Proton
  audio symptom - does not occur here.
  NOT PROVEN: MIDI (dmusic.dll path) specifically. The music playing here is the MP3
  path; a MIDI track was not isolated. Also not proven: per-effect attribution, gain
  staging / clipping, and positional 3D audio.
  NOT a substitute for: a human listening test. A peak meter cannot detect distortion,
  channel swap, or wrong pitch/rate.
