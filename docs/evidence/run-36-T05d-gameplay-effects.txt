========================================================================
ANALYSIS - what this does and does not establish
========================================================================

NEGATIVE RESULT FIRST (important, rules out an earlier assumption)
  run-35 captured the Battle setup screen under effects-off vs effects-on and got
  BYTE-IDENTICAL PNGs (same md5 bdc812c8..., 0/1228800 pixels changed).
  Conclusion: the Battle setup screen does NOT apply post-processing. It is a UI screen
  with a stage preview thumbnail, not a rendered 3D scene. Using it as the comparison
  surface - the original plan for T-05c - was wrong. That is now known, not assumed.

REPLAY RECORDING FAILED (run-34)
  Pressing F2 and playing 20 s produced 0 .rep files in replay\. The exact in-match
  precondition for recording was not identified, so a frozen camera could not be used.

MITIGATION USED
  Capture a burst of 4 frames per profile immediately after the "Battle Start" banner
  clears, from the deterministic spawn camera pose, with NO movement input. The first
  frame of each burst is the most comparable pair because the camera has had the least
  time to drift.

MEASUREMENTS
  Burst file sizes (a proxy for scene complexity / entropy):
    off:  1375892, 1828148, 1643828, 1311977   -> range 1.31-1.83 MB, WIDE spread
    on :  1370643, 1351016, 1357640, 1400958   -> range 1.35-1.40 MB, TIGHT cluster
  Effects-on frames are markedly more uniform in size. That is the expected signature
  of stronger smoothing post-processing (blur/DoF/bloom reduce high-frequency detail
  and therefore PNG entropy), but it is a PROXY, not proof.

  Matched-frame pixel diff, off-1 vs on-1 (first frame after match start):
    3.67% of pixels changed (45117/1228800)
    changed rows concentrate in y61-372 - the upper 3D scene band - NOT the HUD strip
    at y0-60 or the bottom dialogue band.

  For contrast, off-4 vs on-4 (4th frame, camera may have drifted) differs by 31.6%,
  which is consistent with camera movement rather than effects. This is exactly why the
  first-frame pair is the one to read.

VISUAL CHECK of diff-1.png
  The diff is NOT confined to HUD digits. It includes a large curved magenta arc across
  the upper 3D scene and scattered changes along the floor grid lines - i.e. changes in
  rendered geometry/lighting, not just clock text. HUD differences present too (timer,
  Danger warning), which are expected because the timer differs by design.

VERDICT: PARTIAL - attributable difference on a matched frame, not a controlled proof
  * PROVEN: the effects settings change the rendered image in the 3D scene region while
    the camera is nominally identical (3.67%, upper-scene band, arc-shaped regions).
  * NOT PROVEN: which effect causes which visual change. All six toggles were flipped
    together, so this does not isolate HDR vs DoF vs outline vs motion blur.
  * NOT PROVEN: that the result is free of camera drift. Without a replay the camera
    could not be frozen, and 3.67% could partly be a small camera settle.
  * The honest reading: this is strong evidence that the effect pipeline is active and
    alters the scene, not a rigorous per-effect measurement.

WHAT WOULD CLOSE IT
  Either (a) find the in-match precondition that makes F2 record .rep files, or
  (b) toggle ONE effect at a time across many runs and look for a consistent signature
  in the upper-scene band. (b) is cheap and needs no replay.
